# Module-8-Journal-DriverPass

The DriverPass project was all about designing a cloud-based website for a client named Liam, who owns a driving school. He wanted to replace his manual paperwork and phone bookings with an online system that lets students schedule their own lessons, take practice quizzes, and get automatic updates from the DMV. Looking back at the final design, I think I did a particularly good job on the flowcharts. I placed the workflow bubbles strategically from top to bottom so they are clean and easy to follow, making sure the lines don't cross and creating clear paths to prevent double-booking cars or instructors.

If I could go back and revise one part of my design, I would spend more time on the data boxes in the class diagram. I would improve it by breaking down the billing and payment sections into more detail, showing exactly how the system handles different lesson packages and credit card charges. During this project, I realized that understanding what the user needs is the most important part of software design. You have to consider their real-world rules such as making sure the website never allows more than 10 lessons at a time since the business only has 10 cars and 10 drivers. In the future, my approach to designing software will always start with sketching out simple user paths first to ensure the technical setup matches how the business actually runs day-to-day.
